The brake pedal is the upgrade many sim racers make after their first wheel. Below: what a load cell pedal does, what the makers say it changes, and which sets fit your wheel base, console and budget. Every spec, platform and price below comes from the brands' own pages. Prices are the regular prices in euros; where a store showed a sale without an end date, we list the regular price.

## What is a load cell pedal?

A load cell is a force sensor. Fanatec puts it simply: "A load cell brake system measures pressure rather than travel." Thrustmaster says the same of its Raceline LC brake, which "measures the pressure applied to the pedal, rather than the pedal's travel".

Most entry pedals measure position instead. Older sets use a potentiometer; newer ones a Hall sensor. Fanatec describes Hall sensors as "measuring pedal position with magnets", so the system is "contactless, meaning no wear on the sensor". Many load cell sets combine both: Hall sensors on throttle and clutch, a load cell in the brake.

## Are load cell pedals worth it?

What the manufacturers promise is consistency. Sim-Lab explains: "you learn to brake by pressure, which makes it easier to repeat the same braking input lap after lap." Fanatec says the pedal feel "is much more like braking in a real car", and Logitech G sums it up as "Better muscle memory."

Two practical points before you buy:

- **Mounting.** A brake that wants real force pushes the pedals away from you. Logitech G's setup guide for the PRO Racing Pedals says that for pedals on the floor, "30kg is likely the maximum, unless the pedals are secured". Plan a cockpit, a wheel stand or at least a wall brace.
- **The feel is adjustable.** Every set below lets you change the brake feel, with software, elastomers or springs. Fanatec's CSL and ClubSport sets, the Logitech G PRO Racing Pedals and Thrustmaster's Raceline LC sets also let you set the force needed for full braking.

No manufacturer publishes a lap time gain you can check, so we don't give one.

## Which pedals work with your wheel base and console?

On PC, most of these sets also run on their own via USB, next to any wheel base. Moza says the CRP2 "connects directly to PC via USB" and works "regardless of the brand of your wheel base or shifter". Fanatec says the ClubSport Pedals V3 work with any brand's wheel on PC "if the game supports a second controller".

On consoles, the pedals must go through a licensed wheel base:

- **PlayStation 5:** Fanatec's CSL Elite Pedals V2 work on PS4 and PS5 "if connected to a PlayStation-licensed Fanatec Wheel Base". For Logitech G, "Console compatibility requires connection to a compatible Logitech G Wheel". Thrustmaster's Raceline Pedals III LC and LC Upgrade work on PS5 "with Thrustmaster Force Feedback racing wheel bases". Moza does not list PS5 for these pedals; its first PS5 bases still await Sony's final approval (see [Moza's PS5 wheels](/blog/ps5-direct-drive-wheels-2026/)).
- **Xbox:** Fanatec pedals need a compatible Fanatec base "combined with an XBOX-licensed Fanatec Steering Wheel". Moza lists the CRP2 for "PC and Xbox systems". Logitech G and Thrustmaster again work through their own bases.
- **Logitech G29, G920 or G923:** Logitech says "Connection to G29/G920/G923 requires Racing Adapter". On PC, a USB pedal set can run next to these wheels in games that support a second controller.

Fanatec warns that the ClubSport Pedals V3 will "not work on consoles using the direct USB Connection". Already own a base? Our [Moza vs Fanatec comparison](/wheel-bases/moza-vs-fanatec/) lists what each ecosystem connects, and the [wheel base comparison](/wheel-bases/compare/) shows platforms for six brands.

## Hydraulic and active pedals: the step after load cell

Above load cell sit hydraulic and active pedals. Asetek's Invicta pedals use the "T.H.O.R.P. II hydraulic brake system". Active pedals add a motor: Moza's mBooster Active Pedal (€899.00) has "realistic dynamic force feedback", and the Simucube ActivePedal Pro (€1,682.40, one pedal) delivers "1100 N (110 kg) of force feedback".

- **Under €200:** Logitech G RS Pedals (€149.99), Moza SRP2 (€159.00) and Fanatec CSL Pedals LC (€179.90). Own Thrustmaster Raceline pedals? Thrustmaster sells the LC Upgrade kit for them (€139.99), out of stock on 4 November.
- **€200 to €400:** Thrustmaster Raceline Pedals III LC (€219.99), Fanatec CSL Elite Pedals V2 (€299.95), ClubSport Pedals V3 (€329.95), Logitech G PRO Racing Pedals (€369.99) and Sim-Lab XP1 (€399.00 without clutch).
- **€400 to €600:** Moza CRP2 (€449.00), Asetek Forte (€487.05), Sim-Lab XP1 with clutch (€549.00) and Fanatec Podium Pedals Sport (€599.95).
