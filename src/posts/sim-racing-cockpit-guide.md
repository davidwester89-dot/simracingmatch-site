A sim racing cockpit holds your wheel base, pedals, seat and often your screens in one fixed position. Choose well and you buy it once. Choose too light and the frame moves every time your direct drive base kicks or you stamp on a load cell brake. This guide walks through the frame types, rigidity, the parts that bolt on, space, and what you pay. Every spec and price comes from the maker's own product pages: Sim-Lab's EU store, read on 16 November with Dutch prices in euros, taxes included.

## Profile rig, tube frame or wheel stand

There are three common ways to hold a wheel and pedals off the floor.

- **Aluminium profile rig.** A frame of slotted aluminium extrusions, bolted together. You can move the wheel deck, pedal plate and seat along the slots, and bolt accessories anywhere on the profile. All Sim-Lab cockpits in this guide are built this way.
- **Tube frame.** A welded or bolted tube chassis. It adjusts differently from slotted profiles, so check the adjustment range of the wheel and pedal mounts before you buy.
- **Wheel stand.** A lighter stand that holds the wheel and pedals in front of a chair or sofa. It takes the least room, but there is no seat on the same frame, so the chair can roll back under hard braking.

Which one suits you comes down to how much force your base and pedals put out, and how much room you have. That is what the next sections are about.

## Rigidity and direct drive torque

The stronger the base, the more force goes into whatever it is bolted to. If the frame flexes, you lose detail in the force feedback and the wheel can move under load. Wheel base makers warn about this themselves; our [torque guide](/wheel-bases/how-much-torque/) quotes them per torque level.

Sim-Lab lists a maximum wheel base torque on each cockpit page. Here is what it publishes for its current cockpits:

- **GT1 Evo:** 40 x 80 mm profiles, max wheel base torque 40 Nm, max driver weight 100 kg, weight 39 kg. Sim-Lab lists "More than enough rigidity for most wheels" and sells direct drive upgrades separately.
- **GT1 Pro:** 40 x 80 mm profiles, max wheel base torque 40 Nm, max driver weight 200 kg. Front mounting brackets are included next to the wheel deck.
- **P1X Pro and P1X Ultimate:** a 160 x 40 mm base, 120 x 40 mm uprights, max wheel base torque 40 Nm, max driver weight 200 kg.

So on paper, all four take a 40 Nm base. The difference is in the frame around it: thicker base profiles on the P1X, a wheel deck or front mount, and how the pedal deck is built. If you run a strong base or plan to upgrade, read the [direct drive vs belt](/wheel-bases/direct-drive-vs-belt/) guide first and size the rig for the base you will end up with, not the one you start with.

## Seat, monitor mount and pedal plate

A rig is more than the frame. Check three parts before you order.

- **Seat.** On all Sim-Lab cockpits the seat is optional. You can add bucket seat brackets and a seat slider. A slider lets you move the seat for different drivers without loosening the frame.
- **Monitor mount.** Sim-Lab sells an integrated single or triple monitor mount per cockpit. A mount on the rig moves with the rig, so your screen distance stays the same every session. That matters for your field of view.
- **Pedal plate.** Load cell pedals measure force, so you push hard on the brake. The pedal deck must not flex under that force. The GT1 Evo has a pedal tray with adjustable height and angle. For the brake itself, see our guide to [load cell pedals](/blog/load-cell-pedals/).

Also look at the side mounts. The GT1 Evo, GT1 Pro and P1X cockpits all come with a side mount for a shifter or handbrake.

## Space and foldable options

Measure your floor before you look at prices. Sim-Lab lists these footprints:

- **GT1 Evo:** 1350 x 580 x 660 mm (L x W x H).
- **GT1 Pro, P1X Pro and P1X Ultimate:** 1350 x 580 mm, 680 mm at the widest point, 770 mm high.

Add room for the seat sliding back, for getting in and out, and for triple screens if you plan them. A triple screen mount makes the rig much wider than the frame.

The Sim-Lab cockpits in this guide are bolted profile frames, and Sim-Lab lists no folding option for them. If your rig has to disappear after every session, a foldable rig or a wheel stand is the other route. Check the same points there: the maximum torque the maker allows, and whether the pedal plate stays put under braking.

## Which rig fits you

Using only the facts above:

- **Small space, belt or entry wheel:** a wheel stand or a compact rig. Check the maximum torque the maker allows.
- **First direct drive base, tight budget:** the GT1 Evo is the lowest-priced profile cockpit here, at €379.00 bare, with a 40 Nm limit. Note the 100 kg driver weight limit.
- **Strong base, heavy braking, accessories:** the GT1 Pro or a P1X. Both carry a 200 kg driver weight limit, and the P1X has the thicker base profiles and a front mount option.
- **Triple screens:** pick the rig with the triple mount from the start, and plan the floor space for it.
