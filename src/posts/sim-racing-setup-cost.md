A sim racing setup is more than a wheel. You need a wheel base, a steering wheel, pedals, something to mount it all on, and a screen. Below: what each part costs, three example setups with a total, and where to spend first. Every price comes from the makers' own EU stores, in euros incl. VAT. Where a store showed a sale without an end date, we list the regular price.

## What a full setup includes

- **Wheel base:** the motor that drives the wheel and creates force feedback.
- **Steering wheel:** clips onto the base. Some sets ship base and wheel together.
- **Pedals:** throttle and brake, sometimes a clutch.
- **Mount:** a desk clamp, a wheel stand or a cockpit.
- **Screen or VR:** the monitor, TV or headset you already have, or a new one.

Bundles save you from matching parts yourself. Separate parts let you upgrade one piece at a time.

## Wheel base and wheel

The base is the biggest single cost. Our [wheel base comparison](/wheel-bases/compare/) lists 27 direct drive bases from six brands. Their regular prices run from €299.99 to €3,241.59: 6 bases under €400, 12 between €400 and €800, and 9 above €800. Those prices are for the base only.

Three examples from brand stores:

- **PXN V10 Pro Bundle:** €259.00. PXN lists a "3.2 Nm dual-motor direct-drive" base with a 270 mm wheel and magnetic pedals in the box, for PC, PS4, Xbox One and Xbox Series X|S.
- **PXN VD6 Bundle:** €419.00. A 6 Nm direct drive base (7 Nm peak), a 280 mm wheel and a 2-pedal set with Hall sensors, for PC.
- **Asetek Initium:** €354.87 for the base (5.5 Nm, PC) and €151.50 for the Initium Steering Wheel (PC). Asetek shows prices excl. VAT; we add 21% Dutch VAT (our sum).

How much torque you need is in [how much torque do you need?](/wheel-bases/how-much-torque/). Gear, belt or direct drive? See [direct drive vs belt](/wheel-bases/direct-drive-vs-belt/). On PS5, check the platform first: our [PS5 direct drive article](/blog/ps5-direct-drive-wheels-2026/) covers that.

## Pedals

Many bundles include pedals. A load cell brake, which measures pressure instead of travel, is the usual first upgrade.

- **PXN PD HM:** €79.99 for throttle and brake with Hall sensors.
- **Sim-Lab XP1:** €399.00 for throttle and brake with a load cell, €549.00 with clutch.
- **PXN Vector X:** €519.00 for throttle and brake, with a load cell and an angle sensor.

All sets with brake force and platform are in our guide to [load cell pedals](/blog/load-cell-pedals/).

## Cockpit or wheel stand

A desk clamp costs least, but a strong base or a load cell brake will move a desk or a chair. A wheel stand or cockpit keeps everything in place.

- **PXN A10 wheel stand:** €99.99, steel and foldable.
- **Sim-Lab GT1 Evo:** €379.00 for the bare aluminium profile cockpit, rated for 40 Nm.
- **Sim-Lab P1X Pro:** €789.00 for the bare cockpit.

A seat is extra on Sim-Lab's cockpits. Frame types, rigidity and space are in the [sim racing cockpit guide](/blog/sim-racing-cockpit-guide/), and the P1X pair in [Sim-Lab P1X Pro vs Ultimate](/blog/sim-lab-p1x-pro-vs-ultimate/).

## Screen or VR

None of these three brands sells screens or VR headsets, so we give no price for them here. Start with the monitor or TV you already have. If you need a mount for it, Sim-Lab sells the GT1 Integrated Vario monitor mount for €129.00 and a freestanding Vario monitor mount for €279.00.

## Three budget tiers

Each total is our sum of the regular prices above. A screen is not included.

- **Budget, about €359:** PXN V10 Pro Bundle (€259.00) on a PXN A10 wheel stand (€99.99) = €358.99. Base, wheel and pedals come in one box.
- **Mid-range, about €800:** PXN VD6 Bundle (€419.00) in a Sim-Lab GT1 Evo cockpit (€379.00) = €798.00. Add the Sim-Lab XP1 load cell pedals later for €399.00.
- **High-end, about €2,973:** Asetek Forte base (18 Nm, €964.95), Asetek Forte Formula Pro Steering Wheel (€670.07), Sim-Lab XP1 load cell pedals with clutch (€549.00) and a Sim-Lab P1X Pro cockpit (€789.00) = €2,973.02. Asetek prices are our sum: excl. VAT + 21%.

Before you order across brands, check the makers' compatibility lists for mounts and platforms. Sim-Lab publishes one per cockpit.

## Where to spend first

1. **Platform.** A base that does not run on your PC or console is no bargain. Check this before price.
2. **Base and mount together.** More torque needs a firmer mount. A 5 to 8 Nm base is a common start; check what your desk, stand or cockpit can hold.
3. **Brake.** A load cell brake is the upgrade many drivers make after their first wheel.
4. **Screen and seat last.** Use what you have until the rest is sorted.
